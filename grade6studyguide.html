How the Internet Works (Networks)

  • Client vs. Server: Your laptop, tablet, or phone is the Client (the customer asking for a webpage). A Server is a giant, super-powerful computer far away that stores websites and sends them to you when you ask.
  • IP Address: Every device on the Internet gets a unique number code called an IP Address. Think of it like your house's street address so information knows where to go!
  • DNS (Domain Name System): The Internet's Phonebook. Humans remember names like google.com, while computers use numbers. DNS translates website names into IP address numbers automatically.
  • Router: The Traffic Director of the Internet. It looks at data packets and guides them along the fastest road to reach the right device.
  • Bandwidth vs. Speed (The Highway Analogy): Bandwidth is how wide the highway is (how many lanes of traffic can fit at once). Speed is how fast the cars are driving. If 30 students download giant videos at the exact same time, the highway lanes get jammed!
2

Data & File Sizes

Computers measure information from tiny pieces up to massive storage drives. Here is the ladder from smallest to largest:

1. Smallest
Byte
1 single letter (like "A")
2.
Kilobyte (KB)
A short story or email
3.
Megabyte (MB)
1 song or a clear photo
4.
Gigabyte (GB)
A full HD movie or video game
5. Largest
Terabyte (TB)
A giant hard drive (1,000 GB)

Zip Files (.zip): Packing a folder into a .zip file squishes the files together so they take up less space and send much faster over email or the Internet—just like vacuum-sealing clothes into a suitcase!

3

Smart Typing & Keyboard Shortcuts

  • Touch-Typing & Home Row: Keep your fingers resting on the Home Row keys (ASDF and JKL;). Feel for the tiny raised bumps on the F and J keys so your index fingers can find their spot without looking down!
  • Must-Know Shortcuts:
    Alt + Tab or CMD + Tab — Switch quickly between open apps
    Ctrl + Z or CMD + Z — Undo a mistakes one step at a time
    Ctrl + Y or CMD + Y — Redo steps done with CTRL + Z
    Ctrl + F or CMD + F — Find a specific word on a page
    Ctrl + C or CMD + C — Copy selected letters or words
    Ctrl + V or CMD + V — Paste letters or words that were copied
    Ctrl + A or CMD + A — Select All words on a page
4

Cybersecurity & Staying Safe Online

  • Social Engineering & Phishing: Scammers try to trick people using fear or urgency ("URGENT! Your account will be deleted in 1 hour!"). Always check the sender's email address for sneaky spelling tricks (like omololuschoo1.org using the number 1 instead of the letter l). Fake text messages are called Smishing.
  • Strong Passphrases: Instead of a short password like Cat123, build a long passphrase using 4 random words plus numbers and symbols (like Panda#Guitar22$Juice#Orbit).
  • Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Adding a second lock to your account! You need something you know (your password) PLUS something you have (a 6-digit code sent to a phone or authenticator app). Even if a hacker steals your password, they still can't get in!
  • 4 Types of Malware (Bad Software):
    • Virus: Attaches to a file and spreads to other files when opened.
    • Trojan Horse: Pretends to be something fun (like a free game cheat) to sneak onto your computer.
    • Spyware: Secretly watches what you type to steal passwords.
    • Adware: Floods your screen with annoying pop-up ads.
  • Updates & App Permissions: Always install System Updates because they patch security holes. When installing apps, check App Permissions—a simple calculator app has no reason to ask for your camera, microphone, or GPS location!
5

Digital Citizenship & Online Manners

  • Digital Footprint: The permanent trail of posts, photos, and comments you leave behind online. Even if you hit delete, someone may have already saved or screenshotted it. Never share private info (home address, phone number, school schedule).
  • Netiquette (Internet Manners): Be polite and clear online! Avoid typing in ALL CAPS (it looks like shouting), include polite greetings when emailing teachers, and remember people can't see your facial expressions in a text message.
  • Being a Digital Upstander (STOP – BLOCK – TELL): If you see cyberbullying, don't be a bystander who laughs or shares it. Follow the 3 steps: 1. STOP (Don't reply) 2. BLOCK & Screenshot 3. TELL a trusted adult
